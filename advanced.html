Recognize the effect type

Trigger Effects

A listed event makes the effect eligible. Most are Spell Speed 1. If the event happens during a resolving Chain, activate after that Chain finishes.

Optional: “You can” lets you decline. A mandatory effect activates at its next legal opportunity when its condition is met; that does not guarantee it will resolve.

Ignition Effects

Activate during your Main Phase in an open state. It starts a Chain; the opponent may respond.

No “(Quick Effect)” label or listed event? Check the card text and rulings to identify the effect type.

Quick Effects

Labeled “(Quick Effect),” these are generally Spell Speed 2. Activate on either turn when legal; they start a Chain.

Unlike a Trigger Effect, a Quick Effect uses a legal fast-effect window instead of waiting for a listed event.

Continuous Effects

Applies continuously while its conditions are met; ongoing application does not start a Chain. Many apply while face-up, while some apply in other locations.

Activating a Continuous Spell/Trap starts a Chain. After it resolves, its ongoing effect applies without new Chain Links.

Problem-Solving Card Text (PSCT)

PSCT is Konami’s card-text format for showing when an effect can activate, what you do at activation, and what happens when it resolves. Read the current card text; older printings may use earlier wording.

Condition: colon (:)

Text before a colon gives an activation condition or timing. Confirm that it is met before activating the effect.

Activation; semicolon (;)

Text before a semicolon gives activation procedures, such as paying a cost or choosing targets. These happen as the effect is activated, before either player adds a response.

Resolution

Text after the colon and any semicolon describes what the effect does when its Chain Link resolves. Its conjunctions, targets, and any intervening effects matter.

Recognize activated effects

In modern monster PSCT, a colon or semicolon signals an activated effect that starts a Chain. A Spell or Trap Card’s activation also starts a Chain even when its text has neither. Continuous effects and non-activated Summoning procedures work differently.

Example: Solemn Judgment

Solemn Judgment

Condition: a qualifying Summon is being attempted, or a Spell/Trap Card is activated. At activation: pay half your LP. At resolution: negate the specified Summon or activation, then destroy the card if applicable. The payment is not refunded if Judgment is negated.

Costs vs. effects

Before the semicolon: activation

When a card uses a colon and semicolon, the colon introduces the activation condition and the semicolon separates activation procedures from resolution. Actions before the semicolon—such as paying LP, discarding, Tributing, or targeting—are performed at activation when the text requires them.

Those costs are paid even if the activation or effect is later negated. They are not refunded, and opponents respond to the activation—not to the act of paying the cost itself.

After the semicolon: resolution

The instructions after the semicolon are what the effect does as its Chain Link resolves. If a card says “Discard 1 card; draw 2 cards,” the discard is paid at activation and the draw happens only when the effect resolves successfully.

Not every card has a semicolon, and older text may be formatted differently. Verify exact wording in the official card database.

Example: paying a cost

Solemn Judgment

Solemn Judgment requires you to pay half your LP when you activate it. That payment occurs before the Chain resolves; it is not refunded if Judgment is negated.

Targeting vs. non-targeting

Targeting effect

Modern TCG text uses the word target when an effect targets. For an activated effect, you choose a legal target during activation, before the Chain resolves. When a semicolon appears, targeting is usually written before it alongside other activation procedures.

Example: “Target 1 card on the field; destroy it.” You choose the card as you activate the effect. If it moves or changes before resolution, read the resolving text and check the official ruling to see what still applies.

Non-targeting effect

If the effect does not target, you do not select the affected card during activation. The effect’s controller generally chooses a legal card as the effect resolves, following its instructions.

Example: “Destroy 1 card on the field.” With no targeting instruction, the card is chosen during resolution. That can matter if the field changes while players are adding effects to the Chain.

Check the current wordingOlder wording can differ. Check current text and rulings; “choose” or “select” alone does not establish targeting.

Example: Book of Moon targets

Book of Moon

Choose a face-up monster when you activate Book of Moon. If the chosen monster is no longer a legal target when Book of Moon resolves, it is not changed face-down. A Link Monster cannot be placed in Defense Position.

“Then,” “also,” “and if you do,” and “and”

Then

The actions happen in sequence: do A, then B. A must happen for B to happen. If both occur, B is the last thing to happen.

Also

The actions happen at the same time, and neither depends on the other. Apply as much as possible.

And if you do

The actions are treated as simultaneous for timing, but A must happen for B to happen. If A does not happen, stop; if B cannot happen, A still happens.

And

The actions are treated as simultaneous, and both are required. If you cannot do both, you do neither. This wording is less common; use the exact card text and ruling.

Chains & Spell Speed

Spell Speed 1

Includes most Normal, Continuous, Equip, Field, and Ritual Spell activations, plus Ignition, Trigger, and Flip monster effects. These generally cannot be chained to another effect. Simultaneous Spell Speed 1 Trigger Effects are handled in a special Chain-building process.

Spell Speed 2

Includes Quick Effects, Quick-Play Spells, and Normal or Continuous Trap activations and effects. These can generally respond to Spell Speed 1 or 2 effects when their activation conditions and timing allow.

Spell Speed 3

Counter Traps are Spell Speed 3. They can respond to effects of any Spell Speed; only another Spell Speed 3 effect can be chained directly to a Spell Speed 3 effect.

Resolve backward

Players add Chain Links in response to earlier activations. Once both players stop adding links, resolve the Chain from the newest link down to Chain Link 1. Do not resolve an earlier effect before the Chain is complete.

“If” vs. “when”: did the event happen last?

Optional “When … you can”

The listed event generally must be the last thing that happened before the effect gets its chance to activate. If another action happens afterward, the effect may miss its timing.

Optional “If … you can”

These effects generally do not have that same “last thing” restriction. They can usually activate after the Chain finishes if their condition was met and the card remains in the required location. Other activation requirements and rulings still apply.

Last event: drawThe Summon was followed by another action. An optional “When this monster is Summoned: You can …” effect may miss timing. An optional “If this monster is Summoned: You can …” effect can generally activate after the Chain, if its other requirements are met.

Mandatory “When” effects

The usual “miss timing” restriction concerns optional “When … you can” effects. Mandatory Trigger Effects do not use that same restriction, though their trigger conditions and other activation requirements still apply.

Compare two Trigger Effects

“When … you can”“If … you can”

If one effect Special Summons either monster and then draws a card, the draw is the last event. Red-Eyed Dice’s optional “When” effect may miss its chance; Roxrose Dragon’s optional “If” effect can generally activate after the Chain, if its other requirements are met.

Simultaneous Trigger Effects

When several eligible Trigger Effects are waiting after the same event or completed Chain, build a new Chain in this order. Each player orders their own effects within their applicable group.

  1. Turn player’s mandatory effects.
  2. Opponent’s mandatory effects.
  3. Turn player’s optional effects they choose to activate.
  4. Opponent’s optional effects they choose to activate.

After eligible Trigger Effects are placed, players can add appropriate fast effects. Resolve the completed Chain in reverse order. A Trigger Effect still needs its own condition and a legal activation; optional effects may be declined. Official rulebook

Negating an activation vs. negating an effect

Negate the activation

This stops the card or effect from being successfully activated. The Chain Link does not resolve as an activated effect. A response may also say to destroy the card, but destruction is a separate instruction—do not assume every activation negation destroys it.

Negate the effect

The activation was successful, but the activated effect is prevented from applying as it resolves. The card was still activated; the negation changes what happens to that effect.

Costs still matterCosts already paid are not returned just because an activation or effect was negated. “Once per turn: You can use…” and “You can only activate…” restrictions can behave differently after negation—check the exact restriction and official ruling for that card.

Destroying is not negating

Chain Link 1Chain Link 2

If Mystical Space Typhoon is chained to Monster Reborn and destroys it, that does not negate Reborn. A Normal Spell generally resolves even if it has left the field, assuming its target remains legal and no other effect stops it.

Continuous, Equip and Field Spells and Continuous Traps generally need to remain face-up on the field to apply their effects. Destroying one before its effect resolves is different from negating its activation.

When effects can be used

Ignition Effects

Normally activate in an open game state during your own Main Phase. They cannot be chained just because another effect was activated, and cannot be activated on the opponent’s turn.

Quick Effects and fast cards

Generally Spell Speed 2, so they can be used on either player’s turn in an appropriate fast-effect window. Follow specific phase, event, Damage Step, targeting and Set-card restrictions.

Trigger Effects

Wait for the event or timing listed on the card. If it happens during a Chain, finish that Chain first, then determine which Trigger Effects can activate.

Named phase or step

A card saying “during your End Phase” has that timing even though the Battle Phase has an End Step. A Battle Phase End Step and the later End Phase are different parts of the turn.

Full Armor Master: three timings

Your monsterOpponent · Wedge Counter

Take control: if the opponent’s Sudri has a Wedge Counter, the targeted effect is an Ignition Effect for your Main Phase only—not the opponent’s turn.

Place a Wedge Counter: the card describes applying it when an opponent’s monster effect resolves.

Destroy monsters with Wedge Counters: the separate optional effect is used during your End Phase, not the Battle Phase’s End Step.

Summon attempts and effect resolution

A Normal Summon or an applicable Summon procedure does not itself start a Chain. A card that says it negates a monster’s Summon can respond in the appropriate window before that Summon succeeds.

A monster Summoned while a card or effect is resolving is different: players cannot interrupt a resolving Chain. To stop Monster Reborn with Solemn Judgment, respond to Monster Reborn’s activation, not to a Summon partway through resolution. Other cards may respond after a successful Summon if their conditions are met.

Example: stop Monster Reborn

Chain Link 1Chain Link 2

Solemn Judgment can chain to the activation of Monster Reborn. If Judgment resolves successfully, Reborn’s activation is negated. Judgment cannot be activated in the middle of Reborn’s resolution to negate the resulting Summon.

Damage Step timing

  1. Start: apply effects that specifically activate at the start of the Damage Step.
  2. Before damage calculation: flip an attacked face-down monster face-up; eligible ATK/DEF modifiers can be used in the proper window.
  3. Damage calculation: compare ATK/DEF, inflict battle damage and determine destruction. A monster determined to be destroyed is not sent to the Graveyard yet.
  4. After damage calculation: apply effects appropriate to this timing, including Flip effects from a monster revealed by the attack.
  5. End of the Damage Step: send monsters destroyed by battle to the Graveyard, then handle effects triggered by that event. Return to the Battle Step afterward.

Activations are restricted throughout the Damage Step. Examples of permitted categories include Counter Traps, some effects that negate activations, effects that directly modify ATK/DEF in their allowed window, and effects whose text or ruling specifically permits that timing. Not every Quick Effect or Quick-Play Spell is legal. Check the exact substep and card ruling.

Example: compare ATK

Attacker · 3000 ATKDefender · 2500 ATK

At damage calculation, 3000 ATK attacks 2500 ATK: Dark Magician is determined to be destroyed, and its controller takes 500 battle damage. It is sent to the Graveyard at the end of the Damage Step, not immediately during the calculation.

Official Damage Step breakdown

Destroying, sending and banishing

Destroy

Destruction is a specific game action. A destroyed card normally goes to the Graveyard unless another rule or effect changes its destination. Being Tributed, used as material or returned to the hand is not automatically destruction.

Send vs. cost

Cards can be sent to the Graveyard by an effect, as a cost, by battle, or by game rules. An effect requiring a card to be sent “by card effect” does not automatically trigger when it was discarded as a cost.

Banished cards

Each player has their own banished cards. Banishing is not sending to the Graveyard; face-down banished cards have additional information restrictions.

Extra Deck and materials

A face-up Pendulum Monster that would be sent from the field to the Graveyard normally goes face-up to its owner’s Extra Deck instead. Xyz Materials are attached cards; detaching one is not the same as sending a monster from the field.

Once per turn and restrictions

Read the exact wording: a limit printed as “once per turn” can apply differently from a restriction naming a card or effect. “You can only activate” and “you can only use” may differ when an activation is negated; do not assume every negation resets a limit.

Some restrictions apply for the entire turn, others only after an effect resolves, and some only while a card remains face-up. For example, Branded Fusion restricts Extra Deck Special Summons to Fusion Monsters for the turn you activate it. Check restrictions before performing an earlier summon as well as afterward.
